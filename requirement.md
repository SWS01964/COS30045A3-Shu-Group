Hospitalised Injuries from Road Crashes in Australia
Background
Road traffic accidents represent a significant public health issue in Australia. They are consistently identified as one of the leading causes of injury-related hospitalisation nationallyLinks to an external site.. Each year, tens of thousands of individuals are admitted to hospital due to injuries sustained in road traffic accidents. While fatalities often receive substantial media and policy attention, non-fatal injuries constitute a considerably larger and ongoing burden on individuals, families, communities, and the healthcare system.

Australian government agencies publish detailed, open-access datasets documenting hospitalised injuries resulting from road traffic accidents. These datasets include injury counts by year, jurisdiction, demographic characteristics, road user type, and selected population groups. 

Project Aim
The aim of this project is to critically analyse and visualise trends related to hospitalised injuries from road traffic accidents in Australia.

Students are expected to:

Conduct independent exploration of the dataset
Identify and articulate substantively meaningful patterns, trends, or contrasts
Design and implement visualisations that effectively communicate analytical insights
Construct a structured narrative supported by empirical evidence and presented as an interactive web visualisation.
This project emphasises data processing, exploration and analysis and purposeful visual design. It is not intended to produce an unconstrained exploratory dashboard enabling users to view every possible data combination. Rather, the focus is on developing a data story supported by appropriate visual representations.

Team Design Task
Working in teams of two, you will design and implement one or more interactive data visualisations intended for a web-based audience. Your visualisation(s) must:

Present a clear and analytically grounded narrative about hospitalised road crash injuries
Enable users to explore selected dimensions of the dataset in a structured and meaningful way
Demonstrate considered design decisions regarding layout, interaction, and visual encoding, and
Support both exploratory analysis and explanatory communication.
In addition, your team is required to:

document your data processing and design process
conduct and document a usability evaluation of your visualisation using appropriate usability testing methods. 
You may draw on relevant domain literature and supplementary datasets to contextualise and interpret observed patterns. However, the primary emphasis of this assessment is on identifying, analysing, and communicating meaningful insights grounded in the hospitalisation data itself.

Dataset
The primary dataset for this project is as follows:

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE) Hospitalised Injury Data:

https://www.bitre.gov.au/publications/ongoing/hospitalised-injuryLinks to an external site.

The dataset comprises:

National hospitalised injury counts (2011–2021)
State and Territory hospitalised injury counts (2011–2021)
First Nations Peoples hospitalised injury counts (2011–2021)
An accompanying Data Dictionary

Although summary tables are provided in the data files, you are strongly encouraged to derive your own aggregations and transformations directly from the raw data where appropriate (see last tab of each data file).

BRITE has developed a Power BI application (“Hospitalised injuries from road crashesLinks to an external site.”) to help users explore some aspects of the data set. You will be required to undertake a critical analysis of this dashboard for the Critique Assignment. However, this application should be treated as contextual reference only. Its structure and scope are limited, and replication of its design or purpose is neither required nor encouraged in your project.

Note on Interpreting Count Data
The BITRE dataset reports absolute counts of hospitalised injuries. Direct comparison of raw counts across jurisdictions, demographic groups etc may be misleading where substantial differences in population size exist (e.g., Northern Territory compared with Victoria). You are not required to normalise this data to pass this assessment. However, it must be clear in your data story narrative that you are aware of it's limitations.
